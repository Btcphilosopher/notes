1. DR Congo–Central Africa (Katanga & Congo Basin)	Congo Craton margin: Palaeoproterozoic greenstones and Nyong-type belts in DRC/CAR/Cameroon; Archaean Congo Craton gold provinces. Known artisanal gold scattered in alluvium. Major structures (Congo shear zones) cross region.	High (large orogenic potential)	Sparse modern mapping/geophysics, especially under rainforest and volcanic cover.	Remote jungle/plateau; riverine transport; tropical climate. Infrastructure improving around Kinshasa; many regions inaccessible except by air or river.	Instability (eastern DRC conflict persists); weak governance; artisanal mining widespread. Mining code under reform (Congo has new “minière” law).	High biodiversity; indigenous villages; conflict-mineral concerns.	Use satellite (Landsat/ASTER) to map alteration, airborne gravity to find dense zones, soil/till sampling. Test river/stream alluvials for gold nuggets to vector to lodes. Land-clearance free exploration (satellite) is key.
2. Arabian–Nubian Shield (Egypt/Sudan/Saudi)	Palaeoproterozoic accretionary belt (AN-Shield) with prolific orogenic Au, plus VHMS and porphyries. Recent discoveries (Jabal Sayid, etc.) illustrate multi-commodity potential. Thick arid cover in parts.	High (proven endowment)	Large areas in Sudan and Egypt unsurveyed at modern detail. Deeply weathered cover (Egypt, Sudan) obscures veins.	Generally good desert infrastructure (roads, rail in Egypt/Saudi); remote Red Sea hills in Sudan/Egypt tough. Seasonal wadis.	Egypt/Sudan relatively stable, active mineral policies. Saudi intensifying exploration. Maritime boundary/dispute with Ethiopia (Renaissance Dam) is external risk.	Arid, low population density in many zones; but Nile basins and heritage sites need care.	High-resolution magnetics and gravity to delineate buried intrusives and faults; UAV hyperspectral over outcrops; shallow RC drilling of antimony-arsenic anomalies. Joint frameworks (country surveys) to fill geologic maps.
3. Mongolia–Central Asia (Mongolian Altai & Xinjiang)	Phanerozoic accretionary-terranes (Mongolian-Altai orogenic belt) with known Au (Boroo, Oyu Tolgoi Cu-Au). Abundant Trans-Altai belts and huge cratons. Intrusions and metamorphic rocks widespread.	High (numerous targets)	Geophysical coverage uneven; many prospects identified but drilling sparse. Some remote areas.	Harsh climate (cold/dry); sparse infrastructure (Mongolia Gobi; Chinese Xinjiang plateaus). Border controls.	Generally stable governments, mining-friendly codes (esp. Mongolia after 2015 reforms). China frontier areas sensitive (Xinjiang has restrictions).	Nomadic herders' lands; harsh environment. Altitude and desert habitat considerations.	Combine satellite (ASTER ASTER/AWIFS) alteration mapping with broad aeromagnetics. Use trace-element geochemistry on stream sediments. Focus on gold-rich skarns at contact zones, and Carlin-analogue calcareous basins.
4. N. Canada–Greenland (Arctic)	Laurentian cratons and Precambrian shields (Slave, NWT, Labrador, Greenland) contain orogenic and Carlin-like Au. Example: Yellowknife (Mackenzie) and emerging Nunavut belt. Re-entrant geology under ice cover.	Moderate–High (untapped large belts)	Vast areas unmapped (Little or no outcrop under tundra/ice); sparse aerogeophysical data in Greenland.	Extremely remote; short field seasons; costly transport (ice conditions). Some coastal ports (Greenland).	Canada: stable, mining-encouraging (Nunavut’s MUP, etc.). Greenland: new mining law, growing interest but Denmark oversight.	Fragile Arctic ecosystems; indigenous communities (e.g. Inuit, First Nations). Strict environmental regs (ARIA in Canada).	Airborne magnetics/grav in winter (ice skimming), passive seismic for crustal structure. Till sampling (tundra) and LIDAR mapping (where ice retreat exposes rock). Focus on orogenic quartz veins and high-grade fold hinges; new tech (dogs trained to sniff Au?) under development.
5. Scandinavia & Baltics (Fennoscandia)	Archean–Proterozoic shield (Finland, Sweden, Russia) with established districts (Lapland gold, Kola apatite belts with Au). Many glaciated terrains with concealed geology. Recent finds (e.g. Finland’s Kevitsa gold zone).	Medium–High	Glacial cover; many bedrock areas covered by Quaternary drift – need geophysics to “see” beneath.	Good infrastructure. Long winters but well-serviced; EU permitting regimes.	Politically stable; EU environmental/social standards strong. Russia part (Kola) more complex (permit needed).	Sami reindeer pastures; forest conservation; hydropower reservoirs. Eco-tourism areas.	Regional magnetotelluric and grav surveys, dense till/stream sediment grids. Drone LIDAR to detect subtle geomorphology. GPS-RTK mapping of structure (e.g. TSX Sen Lac for orogenic veins). Engagement with Sami councils for access.
6. Amazonian Guiana Shield (Guyana/Suriname/Brazil/Venezuela)	Precambrian craton with greenstone belts (Venezuela’s El Callao) and gold-bearing Archaean granite-greenstone domes. Alluvial placer fields common. Very low formal exploration.	Medium	Dense jungle; very limited mapping/modern surveys. Known small mines, but large-scale geology poorly understood.	Very remote; river travel; environmental preserve constraints (Amazon). Tropical climate, muddy tracks.	Region is politically mixed: stable Guyana (new oil wealth), Suriname stable, parts of Brazil safer (Roraima), Venezuela very high risk.	High biodiversity (rainforest), indigenous Amazon communities, gold-mining mercury issues. Potential conflicts with conservation.	Satellite deforestation mapping combined with geophysics (mag, radiometrics). Rainforest-adapted geochem (bark, soil saprolite). Airborne hyperspectral flown via Honduras/Amazon Basin (NASA’s ECOSTRESS? Hyperspectral from ISS). Focus on hidden lode sources of known placers. Develop dialogue with local miners to co-explore.
7. Southeast Asia (Myanmar/Laos/Cambodia)	Indosinian–Borneo/Mekong orogens: volcano-sedimentary arcs with epithermal and porphyry gold (e.g. Ban Houei, Sepon). Vast fault systems (Red River, etc.) control mineralization. New mines at Wanrong (China)/Pegmatite fields in Laos hint at broader belt.	Medium	Limited data due to past unrest. In Myanmar especially, many unexplored greenstone/altered belts (e.g. Shan State). ASEAN surveys patchy.	Mountainous jungle (Indochina), monsoons. Myanmar infrastructure improving but issues remain.	Myanmar currently high-risk (conflict, sanctions). Laos/Cambodia less risky but have strong government oversight of mining. China border zones sensitive.	Ethnic conflict areas (Myanmar). Precious forest habitat. Artisanal mining pollution.	Use high-res Radarsat (cloud-penetrating) and ASTER to map alteration. Combine IGRF residual with local grid geophysics. Geochemical lake-bed sediment sampling. Work with NGOs to ensure sustainability. High priority: Scout large paleo-eruptive calderas or intracontinental extensional basins.
8. West Australia (Pilbara, Capricorn)	While Yilgarn and Pilbara are well-known, analogous belts (Canning Basin, Pilbara fringe basalts) are underexplored. Recent Tier-1 finds (Telfer, Paterson). Kalgoorlie Superbasin quartzites for orogenic gold, plus Carlin-analogue “Mountain Project” at depth.	Medium	Ground largely staked; limited greenfield left in mature licenses. Potential in basins under cover (Murchison, Canning).	Excellent mining infrastructure; remote desert. Outback roads, rail. Good access (open country).	Very low risk (stable WA mining code). Community (Aboriginal) engagement needed.	arid rangeland; heritage rock engravings. Remoteness reduces human impact.	Satellite gravity (GOCE) + Airborne EM to find buried channels. Continuous reflection seismic (desert conditions allow long lines). Undercover stratigraphic modelling for Paleoproterozoic reefs (gold traps).
9. West Africa (Birimian Belt) (Côte d’Ivoire/Guinea/Mali)	Paleoproterozoic Birimian greenstones host major orogenic Au.  New juniors are expanding margins of Birimian belts. Significant gold in Cambrian cover (SMC gold).	Medium–High (proven belts still underexplored)	Many areas lightly covered by recent exploration. Older data often proprietary. Limited geophysics in interior.	Developing road/rail networks (esp. Côte d’Ivoire, Mali). Seasonal rains cause delays. Some landlocked zones.	Recently improved mining codes (CIV, Guinea). Relatively stable governments (except sporadic coup risk in some Sahel states). Licensing can be bureaucratic.	Savanna environments; less dense population. Land use: agriculture vs mining; artisanal miners present. Upstream water yields mercury risk.	Systematic soil+auger sampling on 800–2000 m grids. Structural interpretation from satellite DEM (SRTM). Airborne magnetics/EM (AusGeophysical survey). Engage CNSI (civil society) on mercury reduction if artisanal. Aim: find under-thrust (“blind”) orogenic quartz veins beneath cover.
10. Carribean & Central America (Colombia/Venezuela/Peru)	Northern Andes and Guiana Shield sutures: orogenic belts (e.g. Pataz, Jaroso), plus epithermal veins (Peru’s Miocene copper/gold). Underexplored highlands in Venezuela/Colombia especially.	Low–Medium	Political instability has limited data; thick tropical cover in places. Remoteness of highland terrains.	Steep terrain; limited roads. Some mines operate (Nevada-Sote, Torrentes in Peru) indicating geology.	Venezuela very high risk (economic collapse). Colombia improving (post-FARC). Others moderate.	Cloud forests; indigenous groups; illegal mining common.	Satellite L-band SAR to penetrate canopy. Drone photogrammetry in accessible canyons. Use local artisanal workings to trace back to lodes. Detailed U/Pb dating of intrusives to target Mesoproterozoic/Phanerozoic gold peaks.
